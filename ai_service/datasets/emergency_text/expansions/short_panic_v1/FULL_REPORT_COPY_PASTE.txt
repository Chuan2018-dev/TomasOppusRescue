B. SEMANTIC COVERAGE GAPS

Existing long descriptions already cover many useful concepts: physical unresponsiveness, absent breathing, heavy bleeding, fire with a trapped occupant, submersion, localized injuries, property-only events, contradictory messages and uncertain timing. What is missing is their short, incomplete, naturally phrased expression and short contextual contrasts.

The dataset has explicit leg/arm pain and impaired movement, but no direct assertion of a broken leg or arm was found. Forehead impact/pain is not equivalent to an explicit head cut. Human death claims occur as uncertain/old forwarded reports; a current scene-linked short human fatality claim is missing. Tagalog already uses patay for an engine in a minor scene, so the engine/person distinction is not entirely absent; it is absent in SHORT contrastive form.

C. CONTEXT AND LANGUAGE RISKS

Keep the transformer/context-aware classifier. Do not add a lookup dictionary that turns patay, dugo, naipit, sunog, unconscious, bali or crash into a label.

The proposed annotation contract is a standalone description submitted through the road/vehicle emergency reporting flow. That channel provides domain context, but does not prove a current accident, a human referent, an eyewitness source or any missing vital sign. Explicit hypothetical, quoted, historical or contradictory wording overrides assumptions about a live observation. Do not use hidden scenario_family, proposed_label or reason fields as model input.

A short fragment can still carry a clear observation. Driver unconscious identifies a person and condition. Dili ginhawa may conventionally convey human breathing failure in this channel; that interpretation is deliberately submitted to fluent Cebuano reviewers rather than made a universal two-word rule. Requiring a literal body-part word in every report would itself be a brittle rule. Equally, context that is genuinely unresolved must not be invented.

Review subject/object (driver versus engine), negation scope (not dead but unconscious), timing (awake before, unresponsive now), evidence source (current witness versus undated forward), uncertainty (murag/daw/kuno/questions), and different victims in one sentence. Words such as lang/ra, awake or walking must not cancel an independently supported danger.

The working minor definition is strict: explicit no injury AND no unresolved danger. Therefore gasgas ra, minor crash, okay ra tanan, damage ra or walay nasamdan alone do not reliably establish minor. A skin scratch is an injury under this rubric; a car scratch is property damage. A stopped bleed does not erase a wound or prove an earlier major loss of blood harmless.
Serious means a concrete injury/symptom without supported immediate life threat in the text. It does not assert that unmentioned breathing, consciousness or circulation are normal. Borderline fragments such as may nasaktan and naipit tudlo need explicit adjudication.
Needs_review is an unresolved assessment, never a low-priority medical conclusion.

D. FATALITY WORDING AND CRITICAL-VS-FATAL CLEANUP

Separate urgency from reported death information.

Proposed optional field:
reported_fatality = none | reported_unverified
Here none means "no affirmative human death claim established from this message," NOT "nobody died" or "the patient is alive."
For ambiguous interpretation, preserve an assessment status such as ambiguous or not_assessed (or null) in a versioned contract. Do not silently treat failed extraction as a confident negative.
reported_unverified records a claim, possibly uncertain or secondhand; it is not a diagnosis, verified outcome or additional severity class. Attach the human referent, wording/evidence span and present/historical/unknown time where supported.

Examples of proposed interpretation:
- patay: needs_review, ambiguous subject. Do not infer human death.
- murag patay: needs_review without a referent/current observation; urgently clarify possible human danger.
- patay na driver, crash just happened: critical as a current human fatality claim requiring urgent verification; reported_unverified.
- Drayber murag patay, dili motubag: critical due to physical nonresponse, with an uncertain human fatality claim.
- patay ang makina: no human fatality claim; severity depends on other condition/hazard evidence, not engine stoppage.
- dili patay, unconscious lang ang driver: critical for unconsciousness; human death is negated, not affirmed.
- old post saying a driver died: reported_unverified when the human claim is clear, but present severity may remain needs_review.

Never return confirmed_fatality from this classifier. Death verification and any later outcome record belong to an authorized human process.

The current Laravel text client maps critical to the legacy stored Fatal value. IncidentReport displays Critical for specified text-model sources, but the legacy value and other sources can still expose Fatal wording. A future separately reviewed migration should establish Critical as urgency across storage, API, UI, filters and analytics, preserve original historical values/source lineage, and keep any reported-fatality observation separate. Never interpret existing Fatal rows as verified deaths or blanket-populate reported_fatality from them.
Relevant current files include AiTextSeverityClient.php, IncidentReport.php, AiSeverityMapper.php and reports/severity.blade.php. None were changed here.

The current four-class model has no independently validated fatality extractor. A future flag needs its own reviewed annotations, negation/subject/time tests and abstention behavior. Do not simulate it using a word search.

H. FUTURE DATASET BALANCE AND TRAINING STRATEGY — NO TRAINING NOW

Do not train on short text alone. A reasonable INITIAL DEVELOPMENT SAMPLING target after human review is approximately:
- 40% short reports of 1–8 words
- 25% medium reports of 9–15 words
- 35% full reports of 16+ words
Within the short part, include both 1–3 and 4–8 words. These are adjustable sampling targets, not validated deployment proportions or a command to manufacture equal counts. Refine them from de-identified real reporter usage when available.

Balance label and language within feasible length strata. Ensure all labels occur at 4–8, 9–15 and 16+ words; create useful 1–3-word injuries and threats as well as uncertain fragments. Do not force a 1–3-word minor quota when the wording cannot naturally establish both no injury and no unresolved danger.
The preview has no 1–3-word minor examples. This is an explicit design limitation, not permission for a length-to-label shortcut. Test the shortest defensible minor phrasings with reviewers and broaden variation at the overlapping lengths.

If all 400 old rows and all 80 preview candidates eventually passed human review and were combined, there would be 480 rows: 68 short (14.17%), 12 medium (2.50%) and 400 long (83.33%). This is only a hypothetical arithmetic comparison, not a merge. The current preview alone would leave the medium-length gap substantial.
Do not multiply paraphrases just to reach a target word count. Expand distinct scenarios, reporters, spelling styles, missing-information patterns and independent incidents. Cap the contribution of repeated variants within a family. Use train-only sampling/weights when appropriate and document their effect on confidence calibration.
Keep natural wording, case, punctuation and negations; do not remove stopwords such as not, dili, wala or hindi. Preserve spelling variants unless correcting a demonstrated data error with traceability. Split families BEFORE any abbreviation, translation, typo or paraphrase augmentation; augment training only. A shortened positive report must be relabeled if its decisive information was removed.
Maintain matched short/medium/long descriptions within the same family, with labels based on what each version actually says. Do not fill the short input with synthetic missing context during inference.
Retain the existing transformer approach for a first future comparison, with a documented baseline and identical independent evaluation. No model switch, training command or release is justified by this preview.

I. SCENARIO FAMILIES AND LEAKAGE PREVENTION

Existing master: 99 group_id values, with translations/related variants sharing groups. The current loader requires the exact eight-column schema and one non-rejected label per group_id. Its split logic stratifies a group using its first row's label. This cannot directly represent a mixed-label contrastive family safely.

Future schema design should distinguish:
1. scenario_family_id: same source incident/seed, translations, paraphrases, abbreviations and counterfactual relatives that must stay in one split.
2. annotation_group_id: only genuinely equivalent observations expected to share a label.
Keep original case_id and legacy group_id in provenance. Do not rename a group merely to make a holdout duplicate look new.
A family may contain minor, serious, critical and needs_review variants after decisive context changes. Enforce label consistency only among adjudicated semantic equivalents, not across all counterfactuals.

For this preview, family_design.json lists proposed SP-Fxx families, candidate membership and old cases requiring a relationship check. A possible old-family link is a review lead, not a claim that every similar symptom is the same incident. Review ancestry, normalized duplicates, near-duplicates, translations and counterfactual origin across ALL candidate/old/frozen datasets.
If a new short description is a shortening of an old test/validation-family report, it cannot move into training. Keep it with that family or exclude it. Preserve frozen split lineage by ID and normalized content/relationship checks.

Contrastive sets sometimes compare multiple SP-Fxx families. Treat intentional minimal/counterfactual transformations as dependency edges and take the connected component as the split unit. Do not cut a component to meet label quotas. Broad thematic examples may be unrelated incidents, but any decision not to link them needs a recorded rationale.
No split assignments are made here. The entire 80-row preview should initially be development/review material, not a source for claiming held-out accuracy. If dependencies yield a large component, keep that component together or obtain new independent evaluation families.
The future splitter must use family-level label/language/length counts rather than the first row's label, save immutable membership and reject overlap. Labels, reasons, family names and IDs are metadata only, never transformer features.

J. DEDICATED SHORT-TEXT EVALUATION PLAN

Create a fresh independently reviewed short-report holdout, plus an independently reviewed 16+ word comparator. Do not treat the existing test set, this 80-row preview, its illustrative probes, or the 32-row rubric calibration pack as untouched short-text test evidence.
Source de-identified real reporting styles where authorized, or independently authored responder scenarios; label them before model evaluation. Fluent language reviewers and a responder should adjudicate uncertainty and borderline cases. Keep the label key hidden from model developers once the test is frozen.

Use two separately reported views:
- A challenge set with useful support for all four labels, languages, short lengths, negation, ambiguity, different victims, death/engine language and changing conditions.
- A natural-use sample when available, preserving actual frequency and uncertainty patterns.
Do not report an artificially balanced challenge set as expected live accuracy.

An initial planning batch could contain roughly 320 reviewed descriptions from at least 80 independent families across the four languages, with a separate longer comparator if it is not included. This is a planning size, NOT a guarantee of precision or readiness. Increase the number of independent critical families until the agreed confidence interval and error tolerance can actually be evaluated. Review availability, observed data distribution and the required uncertainty bound determine the eventual size. Translation variants do not multiply independent clinical evidence.

Report counts of both rows and independent families in every table. Use 1–3, 4–8, 9–15 and 16+ word strata, language strata en/tl/ceb/mixed, and their intersections where supported. Also publish label support. Mark an unsupported cell unavailable; do not silently convert undefined metrics to success.

Required measures:
- Overall four-class accuracy and macro F1, before abstention and after routing.
- Per-label precision/recall/F1 and the full confusion matrix.
- Critical recall = correctly identified critical / all truly critical.
- Critical underclassification to minor or serious / all truly critical, with minor and serious errors separately counted.
- Critical routed to needs_review / all truly critical. This is not a correct severity classification; report it separately from direct underclassification and measure whether timely review occurred.
- Critical routing sensitivity = critical suggestions plus appropriately urgent human-review routing / all truly critical. Report separately; never substitute this for critical recall.
- needs_review predicted-class rate and needs_review precision/recall.
- Operational review_required rate including low confidence, unavailable model and ambiguous/out-of-scope input; this differs from the learned needs_review class.
- Accepted automatic coverage, accuracy/F1 among accepted suggestions, and all-case metrics so a model cannot look good by abstaining on almost everything.
- False critical escalation and responder review burden.
- Each metric by language and length, plus critical error examples and reasons.

Report uncertainty intervals with clustering at the family level; do not treat translations as independent observations. Use validation/calibration families to select any confidence threshold or probability calibration. Freeze all settings before opening the test labels. If test failures cause changes, that set becomes development evidence and a new untouched acceptance set is needed.
Responder-approved acceptance limits for missed critical cases, review delay, review burden, calibration and language parity must be written BEFORE final acceptance. High observed accuracy on a small synthetic slice is not dispatch validation.
If the fatality flag is later added, independently score false positive human death claims, missed claims, negation/subject/time handling and abstention, without treating reported claims as verified deaths.

K. INFERENCE BEHAVIOR RECOMMENDATION — NOT IMPLEMENTED

The current FastAPI request permits 1–1000 characters, subject to blank-input checks. There is no minimum word-count rule. The predictor already routes needs_review predictions, low confidence and over-token-limit inputs to review. Confidence is explicitly marked uncalibrated. It cannot currently substantiate a rich semantic review_reason or independent fatality flag simply by adding a response field.

Preserve the principle: length alone does not force abstention.
- Clear supported short threat: propose critical and route for responder verification.
- Concrete short injury without a supported life threat: propose serious, subject to the validated model and review policy.
- Explicit no injury AND safe/resolved scene: minor may be proposed.
- Missing/ambiguous/contradictory/out-of-scope evidence: no confident severity; needs_review workflow.
- Low calibrated confidence, unsupported input, model failure or input exceeding supported context: abstain visibly rather than guess or silently truncate.

Suggested future response semantics:
severity: minor | serious | critical | null
predicted_label: minor | serious | critical | needs_review | null
confidence: actual model score or null when unavailable; never a fabricated percentage
confidence_calibrated: true only after demonstrated calibration
review_required: boolean reflecting the responder workflow, not just text length
review_reason: supported reason code(s), for example insufficient_information, ambiguous_subject, conflicting_reports, historical_or_hypothetical, low_confidence, model_unavailable, context_limit, or verification_required
reported_fatality: optional independently validated semantic output as described above

Keep learned needs_review separate from operational failure and low confidence. A high score for a concrete class cannot by itself resolve missing or contradictory facts. Any semantic rejection/explanation mechanism requires reviewed annotations and validation; do not add keyword overrides or claim an explanation was inferred when it was only a generic low-confidence fallback.
For a critical suggestion awaiting verification, critical can remain visible alongside review_required=true. For unresolved severity, use severity=null rather than disguising it as minor or confirmed serious. Current Laravel placeholder behavior must be explicitly mapped when this future contract is implemented.
A danger-ambiguous message must stay visible for prompt human contact. Clarifying questions must not block report submission or hide the original text/location. Prefer short checks such as the affected person, current responsiveness/breathing, bleeding amount, entrapment and location. Do not request a longer essay from someone in distress.
Every AI output remains decision support. Do not infer confirmed death, injuries from a photo, normal vital signs from silence, or safety from a model confidence percentage.
