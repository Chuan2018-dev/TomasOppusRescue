# Public linguistic references reviewed

These references informed the decision to treat ?Southern Leyte Bisaya? as a locally variable language variety that needs community review. They were **not** used as sources for the emergency scenarios, and they do not certify the draft wording. The descriptions in the CSV were generated as fictional examples.

1. [UP Diliman Department of Linguistics ? Bisaja, Philippine Indigenous Languages lecture series](https://linguistics.upd.edu.ph/events/bisaja-philippine-indigenous-languages-lecture-series/) ? places Bisaja in Southern Leyte and notes limited dialectological documentation and internal variation.
2. [UP Diliman Department of Linguistics ? Joseph Anavesa and Divine Angeli Endriga do a deep dive on Bisaja](https://linguistics.upd.edu.ph/news/joseph-anavesa-divine-angeli-endriga-do-a-deep-dive-on-bisaja/) ? gives examples of local linguistic features and pronunciation variation. Those examples should not be generalized to every municipality.
3. [EPRA International Journal of Multidisciplinary Research ? study of Binisaja in Agay-ay/Cabalian, San Juan](https://eprajournals.com/IJMR/article/16134) ? discusses locality-specific lexical differences. A word documented in one community is not automatically province-wide usage.

## How to use these sources

Use them to motivate specific local-speaker review, not to ?translate by research? or claim the draft is authentic. Confirm every lexical choice and sentence with fluent speakers from the exact target area.
